# The demo — six clicks, one story

Everything below is driven by the **story rail**, the numbered bar under the
header. Click left to right. Each step drives the screens for you; you do not
need to touch the district selector or the tabs.

**Before you walk in:** have the app open and let it sit for two minutes so the
live board warms itself. Zoom the browser to 100%. Close other tabs.

---

## Open cold (before touching the rail)

You land on the **live board**. Press **Refresh live**, top right.

> *"This is live. Not a recording — I'm pressing it now and the clock resets.
> No API keys anywhere in this codebase; every source is open."*

That earns you the right to be believed for the next five minutes.

---

## 1 · TODAY — "735 districts, monitored"

> *"This is every district in India. Twenty-two run the full physics model;
> the rest are screened on live weather. The distinction is labelled on
> screen — we never dress up screening as assessment."*

## 2 · STANDING — Wayanad, before anything happens

> *"Wayanad. 245 square kilometres of mapped Red Zone. One hundred and five
> thousand people already living inside it. Two habitations flagged for
> immediate relocation.*
>
> *This is true today. It was true in June 2024. Nobody had to wait for a
> disaster for the system to say this."*

## 3 · 30 JUL 2024 — how it knew

The board replays that morning. Point at Wayanad's card.

> *"Cloud column forty points above the five-year normal for that week in
> that place. Soil already saturated. Deepening eleven points every six
> hours.*
>
> *That's not machine learning. Those are published thresholds you can check.
> Every line in that list is the arithmetic that put Wayanad on the board
> that morning."*

## 4 · DISASTER — the exact site

The map flies to Chooralmala-Mundakkai and the justification popup opens by
itself.

> *"This is where the hillside came down. Four hundred and twenty people died.*
>
> *And this is what our model said about this exact ground — before it
> happened. Immediate Red Zone. Cloudburst-driven, five-year return period.
> Against IMD's own hundred-millimetre-an-hour definition and the BIS
> standard the Geological Survey of India uses.*
>
> *We ran this site against our susceptibility map: it scores in the
> ninety-ninth percentile. This was known ground."*

## 5 · RESPONSE — the rescue plan

The hour slider is live. Drag it forward.

> *"Now the same event drives the response. Thirteen thousand affected in
> this zone alone. Four metres of water. Roads cut.*
>
> *Boats, medical teams, shelters — allocated against real capacity, not
> wished for. And when it doesn't add up, it says so."*

If a shelter shortfall line appears, **read it out loud**. It is the strongest
sentence in the demo:

> *"Shelter shortfall of N people once every dry relief centre in the district
> is full. Requisition additional buildings or request inter-district
> evacuation."*
>
> *"That's the system telling a District Magistrate, in writing, that this
> cannot be handled locally. That's what an operations tool does."*

## 6 · AFTER — who is accountable

> *"Detection without an owner is a dashboard. This is the owner.*
>
> *Fifty-seven measures for this district. Every one names the authority that
> leads it and the statute it rests on — DM Act sections thirty and
> thirty-one, RFCTLARR, the Forest Conservation Act.*
>
> *And it says why: relocate rather than protect, because the hazard recurs
> every five years and a protection work lasts twenty-five. It would be
> overtopped several times before it was due for replacement. Twenty-seven to
> fifty-seven crore, order of magnitude, for budget framing."*

---

## Closing line — say it exactly

> *"We can't promise this saves everyone. What it does is put a District
> Magistrate weeks ahead instead of finding out by phone call.*
>
> *A hundred and five thousand people in mapped hazard ground in this one
> district. The exact site of the slide, ranked in the ninety-ninth
> percentile, before it happened. Two habitations flagged for immediate
> relocation.*
>
> *That's not a guarantee. It's a head start — and a head start is what turns
> a death toll of hundreds into fewer."*

Then, thirty seconds, no clicking:

> *"Twenty-two districts run the full model today. Seven hundred and
> thirty-five are screened. Scaling is a data-baking job, not a rewrite —
> one command per district."*

## If asked "is this real satellite data?"

> *"Elevation is real — Copernicus DEM at 30 metres. Built-up surface is real
> — GHSL. Population, cropland, roads and facilities are real. Rainfall,
> cloud and soil moisture are live right now. The Sentinel-1 catalogue query
> is live.*
>
> *What's still modelled is the radar backscatter imagery and river stage —
> there's no public CWC gauge feed — and both are labelled as modelled
> everywhere they appear."*

## If the wifi dies

Nothing changes. All four replay boards, the terrain, red zones, relocation
and mitigation are baked into the build and need no network. Say so — it is
a stronger moment than the live refresh.
