The one idea

The system runs on two clocks

Everything in DRISHTI hangs off this split. Understand it and the whole architecture follows — including why it is affordable to run, which is the question that kills most national-scale proposals.

Slow clock — computed once, then permanent

The land does not change this week

“Where is dangerous?”

Terrain, drainage, red zones. Built from satellite elevation, baked to disk, and served offline forever. Expensive to compute, so it is computed once.

Fast clock — every 30 minutes

The sky changes by the hour

“Where is dangerous right now?”

Cloud structure, rainfall, soil saturation, river response. Cheap to compute, so it runs continuously over the whole country.

The two meet on the Live Board. Rain over empty ground is weather. The same rain over a district where three lakh people already live inside a red zone is an emergency — and only a system holding both clocks at once can tell those apart.

The workflow, stage by stage

From a satellite pixel to “move these 7 villages”

Six stages on the slow clock, one lane on the fast clock. Each stage names what goes in, what it does, and what comes out — so any step can be checked rather than believed.

1

Read the land

core/terrain.py

Water goes downhill. Before you can say anything about floods you need to know, for every 30-metre square of the district, how far above the nearest stream it sits.

In
Copernicus DEM GLO-30 — free 30 m satellite elevation, no account needed
Does
Fills sinks Wang & Liu 2006 → resolves flats Garbrecht & Martz 1997 → routes flow D8 → computes HAND, TWI and slope
Out
Height above nearest drainage, flow direction and steepness for every cell
Why it matters: HAND is the translator of the whole system. It turns “the river is 2 m above normal” into “these houses are underwater.” Verified clean: zero interior pits across all 22 districts — the drainage network actually drains.
2

Make it rain, and route the water

core/rainfall.py → hydrology.py

Rain falls. Some soaks in, the rest runs off, collects in channels and raises the river. This stage does that arithmetic properly instead of guessing.

In
Either a design storm (5-, 25- or 100-year) or live Open-Meteo observed + forecast rainfall
Does
Runoff via SCS Curve Number → routing via unit hydrograph → river stage via Manning compound channel, solved by bisection
Out
River stage in metres, hour by hour — then flood depth via HAND
The key design decision: the seam between “demo” and “live” sits here, at the rainfall object. Swap a design storm for a live forecast and every downstream stage runs unchanged. Live mode is not a second codebase — it is the same physics on different rain.
3

Draw each hazard

flood · landslide · waterlogging · erosion · cloudburst

The problem statement names four hazards. Each gets its own module and its own published method — no single blended “risk score” hiding what actually threatens a place.

Flood
HAND compared against the routed river stage
Landslide
BIS IS 14496 (Part 2) — the Indian national standard — plus the Caine 1980 rainfall intensity–duration threshold
Waterlogging
Topographic wetness index and drainage capacity
Erosion
Shoreline retreat via Bruun 1962
Cloudburst
IMD's own definition — ≥100 mm in one hour — over steep, flashy catchments
Why a national standard matters: the landslide rating is BIS IS 14496, not an invented index. A state engineer already knows it, can already audit it, and already has procedure attached to it. That is worth more than a cleverer number nobody can check.
4

Find the red zones

core/redzone.py

A place that floods once in a century is a place you protect. A place that floods every five years is a place people should not be living. The difference between those two is the entire product.

In
All five hazard layers, each run at 5-, 25- and 100-year severity
Does
Derives a recurrence interval per cell — how often it gets hit — then applies unsuitability thresholds
Out
Red zone map + years-between-hits, per cell, per hazard
This is the PS phrase made literal. “Unsuitable for permanent habitation” is not a vibe — it is a recurrence threshold, published in the code, that anyone can argue with.
5

Count the people — and how exposed they are

core/exposure.py + data/vulnerability.py

Two settlements. Same headcount, same flood depth. One is concrete and literate and connected; one is not. They must not rank equally — that word “vulnerable” in the problem statement's title has to mean something.

In
Population, built-up surface, roads and facilities inside each red zone
Weights
Census 2011 social indicators — literacy 0.35, SC/ST share 0.30, rural share 0.35
Out
Every habitation scored and ranked, with the weights printed on screen
Published weights, not a black box. The priority score is 0.34 exposure + 0.26 frequency + 0.16 unsuitability + 0.16 vulnerability + 0.08 area. A judge can disagree with a weight — which is the point. They cannot disagree with a number they can't see.
6

Say what to do, and who does it

core/relocation.py + mitigation.py

Knowing a village is doomed is useless without somewhere to put it and someone with the legal power to act.

Where
Relocation sites scored on six weighted components, each with a carrying capacity — how many families it can actually hold
When
Immediate / short-term / medium-term horizon per habitation
What
Strategy chosen by comparing hazard recurrence against a 25-year protection design life: protect it, regulate it, or move it
Who
20 named authorities, 15 legal instruments — DM Act ss. 30/31/38, RFCTLARR 2013, CRZ 2019, PMAY-G — and the standing SOPs
The line that closes the pitch: if a hazard returns faster than a structure's design life, you are not protecting that village — you are rebuilding a wall it will outlive. That comparison is what turns this from a map into a decision.
L

The live lane — reading the sky

core/cloudwatch.py → watch.py

Running in parallel, continuously. This is what puts a district on the board this morning rather than in a report next year.

Screen
532 squares over India scored on rainfall, convective energy and soil moisture
Then
Where a square fires, the 22 modelled districts get the full read: cloud column, rainfall vs IMD bands, soil saturation, modelled river response
Rank by
Live weather × standing exposure — the two clocks multiplied together
Also replays history. The same board can be run for Wayanad on 30 July 2024, or the Assam floods of 2022 and 2020 — showing what DRISHTI would have said the morning before. That is the demo that lands.

The defensible centrepiece

Cloud patterns, read — not predicted by a black box

There is no machine learning in the decision path, and that is a choice, not a shortcut. A model that predicts rainfall is a model that has to be believed. DRISHTI reads the vertical structure of the cloud column against textbook convective meteorology and reports what it sees — every step checkable by a meteorologist in the room.

Low/mid/high
Deep convection fills all three levels at once. Ordinary monsoon rain sits low and leaves the high level clear.
High ≫ low
The anvil signature — cirrus outflow from a mature cumulonimbus. This is the cold-cloud shield IMD reads off INSAT.
CAPE
Structure without energy is cloud. Structure with energy is a storm.
Freezing level
A high freezing level means a deep warm-cloud layer — the classic precondition behind Himalayan cloudbursts.
Quiet

Nothing organising.

Building

Column deepening, energy accumulating.

Deep convection

Troposphere-spanning column with CAPE behind it.

Mature system

Anvil spreading. The sky has already committed.

And the anomaly is the point. A deep column over Cherrapunji in July is a Tuesday. The same column over Jaisalmer is not. Every reading is placed against that location's own five-year record for the same calendar week — which is why the board does not fire every day of the monsoon and get ignored by June. The rainfall forecast is reported alongside, from the weather model, and never blended into a single invented number.

Coverage

All 735 districts — with the difference stated on every record

A national map where a weather-only score looks identical to a fully modelled one would be the most misleading thing this system could produce. So it cannot happen: every district record carries its tier and a sentence saying what stands behind the number.

Modelled 22 districts

Full physics — HAND inundation, D8 routing, BIS landslide rating, recurrence across three return periods, exposure and relocation caseload.

Screening 713 districts

Live weather aggregated from the national grid, with no terrain analysis behind it. Says where to look. Is not a hazard assessment, and says so.

Promotion between tiers is a data task, not a code change: bake a district's elevation and it moves up. That is the honest answer to “does this scale?” — the pipeline already handles all 735; what the other 713 are waiting on is disk, not development.

Ask us the hard question first

What DRISHTI does not claim

Every limitation below is stated in the running application, not just in this document. This section exists because the fastest way to lose a technical panel is to be caught overselling one number.

  • River stage is modelled, never gauged. There is no public, documented, keyless CWC feed — three routes were tried and are written up in the code. Stage is routed from live rainfall through real terrain, and every response says so. A modelled stage shown as a gauge reading would be a lie with a decimal point on it.
  • Cloud reading is not a rainfall forecast. It says the sky is organising and how fast. It is never blended with the precipitation forecast into one invented number.
  • Return periods are exceedance bands, not fitted curves. A five-year daily archive holds five annual maxima — far too few for extreme-value fitting. Quoting “a 1-in-80-year storm” from it would be fabrication with a decimal point on it.
  • The disaster register and Census indicators are curated, not live. No free API carries Indian flood history. Every row therefore carries its citation and source URL, visible in the app.
  • Satellite rasters are baked, not streamed. Elevation and built-up surface are fetched once and shipped. This is what makes the app work with the network unplugged — a deliberate trade, not an omission.
